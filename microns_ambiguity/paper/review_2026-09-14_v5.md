# Review: "What the correlations between neurons say about each neuron" (Draft v5)

## 1. Summary

The paper asks whether the pairwise correlation matrix of a sampled neural population, with the stimulus discarded and no neuron labelled at test time, determines each neuron's preferred orientation and receptive-field position. A transformer reads the standardised correlation matrix of a sampled population, one row per neuron plus three row statistics, and emits a per-neuron orientation vector or an $(x, y)$ position. On MICrONS (one mouse, 13 scans, 12,894 co-registered neurons) it reaches $25.4^\circ$ mean angular error in vivo and $20.2^\circ$ on the digital twin against $45^\circ$ uninformed and $41.3^\circ$ for the best constant, with $R^2 = 0.23$ and $0.48$ for receptive fields; the readout is explicitly cardinal (87%/79% correct cardinal axis against a 56% majority, near chance at the obliques). The mechanism section shows that the class-averaged correlation matrix is nearly circulant, which fixes the classes only up to $D_8$, and that a residual anisotropy (29% of class-Gram variance in vivo, 19% on the twin, volume-wide) supplies the absolute frame; the residual survives class balancing, disjoint stimulus halves and neuron-half resampling, and a model population carrying only a shared stimulus-driven component over preferred orientation reproduces its one-peaked shape and fixes the frame in 15 of 15 runs. Across 33 Allen mice, with relations from drifting gratings only, orientation transfers to held-out animals ($\approx 36.9^\circ$) when populations mix animals and is $0.8^\circ$ to $1.8^\circ$ below the best constant when they do not, which the paper attributes to training diversity and supports with a matched single-scan MICrONS run. Since v4 the substantive changes are: a per-area table of label mode and anisotropy peak (V1, RL, AL and the whole volume, both substrates) that makes the coincidence claim checkable, restores the AL twin value v4 had deleted and puts the AL substrate disagreement in the limits; a systematic correction of the vocabulary, so the anisotropy is now described as one of signal correlations and tuning similarity on trial-averaged (twin: noiseless) responses, the model as a shared stimulus-driven component, and the noise-correlation citation is dropped; corrected Allen bounds with the size-matched control quoted in place; labelled volume-wide versus V1 residual fractions; both scalars for both model families; renamed and cross-referenced appendices; and about twenty smaller number and wording fixes. Three requested experiments were declined (a leak-free projection ablation, an Allen permutation null, weaker synthetic strengths). I re-derived the new table and the residual statistics from the repository's data: Table 2's counts, peaks and factors reproduce, with one rounding error, and so do the residual fractions and the same-class excesses.

## 2. Three strongest aspects

1. **The per-area table settles the question v4 left open, and it settles it against the authors.** Table 2 gives, per substrate, the label mode and the anisotropy peak with its factor for V1, RL, AL and the whole volume. I recomputed it from `microns_ambiguity/data` and it reproduces: V1 $3{,}858$ neurons, mode $90^\circ$ (0.28), peak $90^\circ$; RL $1{,}125$, mode $0^\circ$, peak $112.5^\circ$ on both substrates; AL 294, mode $0^\circ$, peak $0^\circ$ on the twin and $135^\circ$ in vivo; whole volume mode and peak $90^\circ$. The claim in the text is now exactly what the table supports — "The coincidence is therefore established for V1 and for the volume" — the AL twin value that v4 had deleted is back and matches the repository's own summary, and the substrate disagreement is named in the limits ("in AL the two substrates place the anisotropy peak $135^\circ$ apart"). A negative result about two areas that was an unsupported assertion in v4 is now a printed number a referee can check.

2. **The vocabulary correction was carried through the whole paper rather than patched in the abstract.** The abstract now says "the neurons preferring the dominant orientation have more similar responses to one another than neurons of any other orientation"; Section 3.2 adds the sentence that does the work — "The quantity is a signal correlation over trial-averaged responses, and on the twin over noiseless ones, so the anisotropy is one of tuning similarity, not of trial-to-trial co-fluctuation"; the Discussion follows; the appendix models it as "a shared stimulus-driven component among like-tuned neurons"; the table caption follows; and `\citet{kohn2005}` is gone from the text, with `\citet{ko2011}` kept in its correct role, "one route by which their responses could become more similar". The paper now claims what it measured.

3. **The numbers that were wrong are right, and the corrections went in the expensive direction.** The Allen paired bound is now $4.3^\circ$ to $7.9^\circ$ with the size-matched control ($36.0^\circ$ at 128 neurons) quoted in the same sentence rather than buried; the abstract's Allen margin is $0.8^\circ$ to $1.8^\circ$ rather than "$1^\circ$ to $2^\circ$", its balanced twin error $24.5^\circ$ rather than "$24^\circ$", and its causal connective is split correctly ("The ordering is recoverable because ... the absolute frame is recoverable only because of a departure"); the $35.7^\circ$/$35.2^\circ$ Allen discrepancy is explained (per-neuron versus population-level scoring of the same run, "32 population draws per neuron"); "about 280 cells, about 170 of them labelled" now matches $9{,}281/33$ and $5{,}529/33$; "not at all" became "barely". Four of these had survived three previous rounds.

## 3. Major concerns

**3.1. Now that both scalars are reported for the model, they disagree about whether the model is weaker than cortex, and the paper's calibration sentences — "cortex lies well above the threshold", "down to a third of the cortical one", "seed-sensitive near the cortical strength" — are respectively unsupported, true on one scalar only, and false.**

v4's minor 15 asked for both scalars for both model families, and v5 supplies them. The local field is now reported as "its size is set by the weight and reported as the residual's fraction of class-Gram variance (V1: 0.16 on the twin, 0.30 in vivo) together with the anisotropy factor (1.67, 1.99 and 1.92 at the three sizes)". Table 2 gives cortex's factor on the same definition: V1 1.28 on the twin and 1.77 in vivo, whole volume 1.26 and 1.81. So the model's *weakest* setting has an anisotropy factor of 1.67, which is 30% above twin V1's and 33% above the whole volume's on the twin, and just under in-vivo V1's. On the residual-variance scalar the same setting is 0.05 against 0.16 and 0.30, i.e. well below cortex. The two summaries of "how strong is the anisotropy" therefore order the model and cortex in opposite directions, and the paper uses only the one that favours it:

- Section 3.2: "from a residual weaker than the twin V1's (0.05 of class-Gram variance against 0.16)";
- Section 3.2: "no strength tested fails, so cortex lies well above the threshold";
- Discussion: "recovers the absolute frame at every strength tested, down to a third of the cortical one".

The second of these is the one I would not let through. No threshold was located — the authors declined to run a failing strength — so "cortex lies well above the threshold" asserts the existence and position of something no experiment in the paper constrains, and on the second scalar the weakest model setting is *above* twin cortex, so even the direction is not established. The honest statement of the same fact is: the frame is fixed at every strength tested, the weakest of which is below cortex on the residual-variance measure and above it on the anisotropy-factor measure, so no threshold was reached and the calibration of the model to cortex is one-sided.

Two further sentences in the same cluster are stale. The limits paragraph still reads "The model result is seed-sensitive near the cortical strength". For the model that now carries the sufficiency claim this is false: Table 6 gives 5/5, 5/5, 5/5 with raw-error ranges of [3--4], [2--3] and [2--2] degrees. The sentence describes v3's Fourier field or the count/sharp switches, not the local field, and a reader who checks the table will read it as a concession the paper no longer needs — or, less charitably, as a limits paragraph that was not reread. And the local field's factor is non-monotone in its own strength parameter (1.67, 1.99, 1.92 at residual fractions 0.05, 0.15, 0.23), which is unexplained and makes the factor look noisy at the one place the reader is invited to compare model with cortex.

None of this touches the sufficiency demonstration itself, which stands: an anisotropy of this shape, with uniform counts and an even stimulus, fixes the frame, and the cardinal-count and cardinal-sharpness models do not. What it touches is every sentence that translates the demonstration into a statement about cortex, and those sentences are in the Discussion and in Section 3.2.

What would resolve it, in order of cost: (a) two runs at weights below the weakest tested, reported on both scalars, which would either produce the failing strength that makes "cortex lies well above the threshold" true or show that the threshold is below anything reachable; or (b) if the runs are declined again, rewrite the three sentences to say that no strength tested fails, that the weakest tested setting is below cortex on the residual fraction and above it on the anisotropy factor, so the model is not calibrated to cortex in a single ordering, and delete or correct "The model result is seed-sensitive near the cortical strength". (b) is four sentences and I would accept it.

Beyond this, I have no major concerns. v4's two majors are resolved, the first by measurement.

## 4. Minor issues

1. **The V1 twin anisotropy factor is 1.28, not 1.29, and v5 changed it in the wrong direction.** v4 printed "1.28 on the twin, 0.071 against 0.055" and my v4 minor 10 objected that the rounded operands give 1.29; the fix should have been to keep 1.28 and add a digit to the operands. Recomputed from the release: peak (90$^\circ$) same-class mean 0.07060, mean of the other seven 0.05516, ratio 1.2799. The value appears twice, in Section 3.2 and in Table 2. Every other entry of Table 2 reproduces to the printed precision (RL 1.223/1.678, AL 1.485/1.687, volume 1.258/1.808, V1 in vivo 1.7651), so this is the only arithmetic error I found in the new table.

2. **The abstract's single-scan range disagrees with the Discussion's.** Abstract: "single-scan populations in MICrONS are decoded to $31^\circ$ to $33^\circ$". Discussion: "at $31^\circ$ to $32^\circ$ on MICrONS". Table 5: $32.0^\circ \pm 1.1^\circ$ (twin) and $31.1^\circ \pm 1.2^\circ$ (in vivo). The two point estimates are 31.1 and 32.0; pick one range and use it in both places.

3. **The two oblique ranges are two different quantities described in the same words.** Section 3.1: "within $15^\circ$ of an oblique, $36^\circ$ to $39^\circ$". Section 3.2 and Appendix B: "$37^\circ$ to $39^\circ$ at the obliques on the twin". Recomputed on the saved predictions, both are right: pooling the neurons within $15^\circ$ of an oblique gives 36.0 and 37.1 (twin, splits 1 and 2) and 38.8 and 38.6 (in vivo), hence 36--39 across substrates; the per-bin $22.5^\circ$-class values on the twin are 36.9 at $45^\circ$ and 39.4 at $135^\circ$, hence 37--39. Fourth round on this point, and it is only readable if each range says which quantity it is ("pooled over neurons within $15^\circ$ of an oblique, both substrates" versus "per oblique class, twin").

4. **The one-peaked description holds in vivo and not on the twin at the block level, and the excesses quoted are V1's inside a volume-wide paragraph.** The sentence is "neurons preferring orientations within about $45^\circ$ of $90^\circ$ correlate more with each other than the circulant baseline predicts, and the excess at $90^\circ$ exceeds that at $0^\circ$ on both substrates ($+0.036$ against $-0.011$ in vivo, $+0.014$ against $+0.006$ on the twin, where the asymmetry is the smaller)". The parenthesis reproduces exactly for V1 ($+0.0362$/$-0.0112$ in vivo, $+0.0135$/$+0.0057$ on the twin), but the paragraph's opening numbers (29%, 19%) are volume-wide, so the switch of population is silent; the volume-wide pair is $+0.033$/$-0.009$ and $+0.011$/$+0.010$, and the twin's ordering is much weaker there. The first clause is the bigger problem: the mean class-Gram residual over the five classes within $45^\circ$ of $90^\circ$ is $+0.0095$ in vivo V1 against $-0.0065$ for the other three classes, which supports it, but on the twin it is $+0.0022$ against $+0.0063$ — the twin's positive residual sits on the $0^\circ$ block, not the $90^\circ$ one. On the diagonal alone the twin's positive same-class excesses are at $90^\circ$, $112.5^\circ$, $0^\circ$ and $157.5^\circ$. So "within about $45^\circ$ of $90^\circ$" describes the in-vivo residual; on the twin only the same-class ordering at $0^\circ$ versus $90^\circ$ survives. Say so, and label the excesses V1.

5. **"Established for V1 and for the volume" counts one instance twice.** V1 supplies 3,858 of the 5,287 labelled neurons, 73%, so the volume row is not an independent confirmation of the V1 row — as the numbers show (volume 1.258/1.808 against V1 1.280/1.765, peaks identical). Of the three areas with enough labelled cells the coincidence fails in RL on both substrates and in AL in vivo. The accurate statement is that the coincidence holds in V1, that the volume the decoder reads inherits V1's peak, and that it does not hold in the two smaller areas.

6. **AL's in-vivo peak is not separated from its neighbours, which makes the "135° apart" limits sentence sound stronger than the data.** In-vivo AL same-class means are 0.138 ($135^\circ$, 40 neurons), 0.131 ($112.5^\circ$), 0.115 ($90^\circ$) and 0.110 ($0^\circ$), over 294 neurons in the area; the twin's peak, 0.204 at $0^\circ$ against a next-best 0.185, is well separated. The limits sentence should say that AL's in-vivo peak is within noise of three other classes at $n = 294$, so the disagreement is as likely a sampling effect as a property of the fit. As written it reads as a substrate artefact of $135^\circ$, which is the least favourable reading available and probably not the right one.

7. **The Allen container criterion cannot be what Methods says.** "From Visual Coding 2P we take the 36 VISp excitatory containers with at least 150 orientation-labelled cells: 33 mice, 9,281 cells, of which 5,529 carry an orientation label." Thirty-six containers at a floor of 150 labelled cells each require at least 5,400 labelled cells and 5,529 are reported, leaving an average of 3.6 cells above the floor across 36 containers. The criterion is presumably 150 cells, not 150 labelled cells; $9{,}281/36 = 258$ is a plausible mean under that reading.

8. **The Allen single-animal margin is stated against a pooled null.** "all six runs lie $1.9^\circ$ to $2.9^\circ$ below chance and $0.8^\circ$ to $1.8^\circ$ below the best constant" uses the best constant of the pooled label distribution ($43.9^\circ$), while each split's test set is nine mice with its own distribution. Report the per-split best constant, which is free, so that the $0.8^\circ$ margin is against the null of the set it was measured on. The permutation null was declined; with it declined, the strongest defensible statement is the one the paper can make from the design — six runs of two regimes on three splits, all below the constant, which is what "weak but consistent" should be glossed as.

9. **The last surviving "co-fluctuation" is a table row.** Table 6's bottom block is labelled "like-tuned co-fluctuation, residual 0.05 of class-Gram var.", while its own caption calls it "a shared stimulus-driven component among like-tuned neurons". Rename the row.

10. **"the most strongly co-correlated one".** The Section 3.2 paragraph head; "co-correlated" is not a word and the paper has a plain phrase for the thing ("whose neurons correlate most strongly with each other").

11. **Two 56%s still share a sentence.** "it is right for 87\% of all neurons on the twin and 79\% in vivo (majority answer 56\%; ...), and for the neurons whose preference lies within $15^\circ$ of an axis, which are 56\% of the population, for 94\% and 86\%". Third round. Moving the population fraction to the following sentence fixes it.

12. **Appendix A does not say which substrate is which.** "only the 0.3M model is worse ($27.5^\circ$ and $27.8^\circ$ on MICrONS)": two numbers, two substrates, no order given. Same paragraph, "Orientation error is flat from 2.2M upward on both MICrONS substrates" quotes only the twin series.

13. **Two TODOs.** "TODO: affiliation and co-authors" and "\paragraph{Code and data.} TODO: repository URL." Fourth round; the second is a submission requirement at both target venues and, given how much of the paper's credibility rests on numbers a reader cannot otherwise check, it is also the cheapest thing the authors can do for this review's kind of scrutiny.

14. **Twenty pages, one more than v4, after a round of cuts.** Fig. 1's caption went from ~280 to 204 words and the bin-shuffled discussion from ~150 to two sentences, but the new table and the added glosses more than paid it back. Still cuttable without losing an argument: the Introduction's fourth paragraph ("We answer it with a decoder ...") restates the Protocol subsection; the selectivity-confound sentence in "What the decoder reads" can be a parenthesis; "The relations carry the readout, and the order-dependent input layer contributes nothing" restates the sentence before it; Fig. 1's caption still repeats the Data paragraph's bin counts, gOSI threshold and neuron counts. The abstract is 343 words, which is over the PLOS limit and long for NeurIPS.

15. **"The per-area errors are within $3^\circ$ of each other" now sits next to a table saying the areas have different anchors.** One clause would remove the apparent tension: the anisotropy supplies a frame for the population as a whole, which is read from a volume-wide sample, not a per-area prior, so an area whose label mode differs from the volume's peak is not thereby mis-framed.

16. **Table 2's caption says LM is omitted; the volume row includes it.** $3{,}858 + 1{,}125 + 294 + 10 = 5{,}287$. "LM has 10 labelled neurons and is not given its own row" is accurate.

17. **Bin definitions given two ways.** Table 2 and Section 3.2 use "$22.5^\circ$ classes" and "the $22.5^\circ$ class centred on $90^\circ$"; Appendix B's class-balance paragraph uses "within $11^\circ$ of $0^\circ$, 25\% of $90^\circ$". Same bins ($\pm 11.25^\circ$), two descriptions, and the 25% has to be matched by hand against Table 2's volume row (0.25) to see that they agree.

18. **`kohn2005` is now uncited but still in `refs.bib`.** Harmless under `unsrtnat`, but delete it so the file records the decision.

19. **The row-statistics sentence interrupts the anisotropy paragraph.** "The three row statistics alone, which include each neuron's mean correlation, fix a weak frame by themselves ($37.7^\circ$ raw and aligned), and the Gram row without them fixes the full one ($20.3^\circ$ ...)" is an ablation result sitting between the definition of the anisotropy factor and its interpretation. It belongs with the fourth observation or on its own line.

20. **The bin-shuffled control still ends on the caveat that empties it.** Two sentences now, good, but "with the caveat that it has to discover the relations under a fresh permutation for every population" means the comparison cannot support the conclusion drawn one clause earlier ("we find no evidence that richer statistics than the pairwise Gram are available"). Either drop the conclusion or drop the control and its two table rows.

## 6. Recommendation

**Minor revision.**

v5 answered v4's two majors the right way round — the per-area coincidence by printing a table whose every number I could reproduce from the release, including the two rows that go against the authors, and the co-fluctuation problem by correcting the claim through abstract, results, discussion and appendix rather than hedging it in one place — and it closed 21 of 30 minors outright. What is left is one cluster of calibration sentences about the model, which the authors can fix with two runs or four sentences, plus a page of number-consistency work; nothing in it threatens a result, and I would not need to see the paper a sixth time.

**What a hostile reviewer 2 would say.** Five points. (i) Every MICrONS claim comes from one animal whose training and test halves share recording sessions, and the multi-animal result exists only for a designed 40-condition grating set — a tuning-profile similarity matrix, closer to classical RSA than to the paper's "discard the stimulus" premise — and vanishes on natural movies, so the headline generalisation and the headline effect never co-occur in one dataset. (ii) The decoder is a cardinal-axis classifier with a graded distance readout: obliques are at chance on both datasets, 63 to 72% of predictions land on two of eight classes, the Allen four-class bar sits on the majority line, and "determines each neuron's preferred orientation" oversells a $25^\circ$ mean. (iii) The mechanism is exclusion plus a synthetic sufficiency demonstration in $n = 1$ animal: the direct ablation leaked labels and was withdrawn, the cross-area rotation was a single-seed artefact and was withdrawn, the anisotropy-to-frame link is conceded to be inferred rather than measured, and the model that carries the sufficiency claim fixes the frame at every strength tested, ten times more accurately than cortex and — on the paper's own second scalar — at an anisotropy stronger than twin cortex's at its weakest setting (my 3.1). (iv) Now that the table is printed, the coincidence that motivates the whole mechanism holds in one of the three areas with usable counts, and the volume row that the paper cites as its second instance is 73% V1. (v) The Allen single-animal effect, which the abstract states as a number, is $0.8^\circ$ against a pooled null with no per-split null and no permutation test. My own view: (i) and (ii) are conceded in the limits and should also be named in the Discussion's opening rather than only its closing paragraph; (iii) is 3.1 and is answerable in four sentences; (iv) is minor 5, one clause; (v) is minor 8, one cheap computation.
