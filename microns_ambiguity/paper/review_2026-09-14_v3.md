# Review: "What the correlations between neurons say about each neuron" (Draft v3)

## 1. Summary

The paper asks whether the pairwise correlation matrix of a sampled neural population, with the stimulus discarded and no neuron labelled at test time, determines each neuron's preferred orientation and receptive-field position. A transformer reads the standardised correlation matrix of a sampled population, one row per neuron plus three row statistics, and emits a per-neuron orientation vector or an (x, y) position. On MICrONS (one mouse, 13 scans, 12,894 co-registered neurons) it reaches 25.4° mean angular error in vivo and 20.2° on the digital twin against 45° for an uninformed decoder and 41.3° for the best constant guess, with R² = 0.23 and 0.48 for receptive fields; the readout is explicitly cardinal — 87%/79% correct cardinal axis against a 56% majority, 12°/19° within 15° of a cardinal axis, 36° to 39° at the obliques, i.e. near chance. The mechanism section shows that the class-averaged correlation matrix is nearly circulant, which fixes the orientation classes only up to the dihedral group D₈, and that a residual anisotropy (29% of class-Gram variance in vivo, 19% on the twin) supplies the absolute frame; the residual survives class balancing, disjoint stimulus halves and neuron-half resampling, and a synthetic population carrying only stronger co-fluctuation among like-tuned neurons fixes the frame in four of five seeds at the twin's measured anisotropy factor (1.32 against 1.28) and five of five at the in-vivo factor (1.75 against 1.77). Across 33 Allen mice, with relations from drifting gratings only, orientation transfers to held-out animals (≈36.9°) when populations mix animals and is 1.9° to 2.9° below chance when they do not; the paper attributes that to training diversity and supports it with a matched single-scan MICrONS run. Since v2 the substantive changes are: the anisotropy factor is defined and the synthetic strength matched to it with five seeds per strength, the cross-area transfer is rerun with extra seeds and class-balanced training, the 78° V1→AL rotation is found to be a single-seed artefact and the cross-area observation is withdrawn from Section 3.2, seeds are added to the within-scan and cross-area rows, the error-by-orientation figure is promoted to the main text, the leaking ablation gets a full appendix paragraph, the limits paragraph is extended to six limits, and citations and numbers are corrected.

## 2. Three strongest aspects

1. **The withdrawal of the cross-area claim is handled the way one wants it handled, and it cost the paper its most quotable observation.** The v2 draft's fifth mechanism observation was the closest thing in the paper to a causal test: "a decoder trained on V1 ... to AL ... with a raw error of $58^\circ$ that falls to $32^\circ$ after one global rotation of $78^\circ$". Rerun with seeds, it did not hold, and the paper says so in plain words — "The single-seed rotation of $78^\circ$ reported in an earlier draft did not replicate, and no claim rests on these rows" — while keeping all three seeds of every row in the controls table so the reader can see the spread that killed it (`trained on V1, scored on RL, raw / aligned (3 seeds each) ... 26, 43, 46 / 25, 41, 39`). Authors much more often quietly drop such a row. This one deserves explicit credit, and it is the main reason I now trust the rest of the numbers.

2. **The synthetic anchor test has become a real dose-response result rather than a switch list.** Defining the anisotropy factor ("the mean correlation between two neurons of the same orientation class, at the class where it peaks, divided by its mean over the other seven classes"), measuring it on both substrates, and then running the model at 1.09, 1.32, 1.68 and 1.75 with five seeds each turns v2's two-seed anecdote into a monotone curve (2/5, 4/5, 5/5, 5/5) whose knee sits below the cortical values. The threshold sensitivity is reported ("unchanged with the raw-error threshold at $25^\circ$ or $35^\circ$, except that the count cell drops to 0 of 5 at $25^\circ$") and the one degenerate run is named rather than averaged in silently ("One run (all three switches, seed 0) never left its initial loss ... it is included in the mean"). This was v2's single largest concern and it is now closed.

3. **The paper states what the readout actually is, in the abstract, in the results, in a main-text figure and in the limits.** "It does not resolve the obliques" in the abstract; "the decoder resolves the cardinal axes and little else" in Section 3.1 with the per-band numbers; Fig. 3 promoted from the appendix; and "The readout is cardinal: the obliques are not resolved on either dataset, so the mean error understates the precision at the cardinals and overstates it elsewhere" in the limits. The bin-averaged coarse accuracies (83% and 74% on MICrONS, 61% on Allen) are now given alongside the raw ones. Combined with the six-item limits paragraph and the frank appendix on the leaking ablation, the draft reads as careful throughout rather than careful-where-convenient.

## 3. Major concerns

**3.1. The paper's second headline claim names a coincidence between two quantities, and only one of them is reported anywhere in v3 — while the paper's own numbers make the coincidence fail for AL.**

The abstract says: "in each area the neurons preferring the dominant orientation co-fluctuate most strongly". The Discussion repeats it: "In each area the neurons preferring the over-represented orientation co-fluctuate most strongly, whether or not the sampled population is imbalanced." Section 3.2's fifth observation is titled with it: "*The dominant orientation of an area is its most strongly co-correlated one.*"

That is a statement about two things: where an area's *label distribution* peaks, and where its *anisotropy factor* peaks. v3 reports the second and, since the cross-area withdrawal, no longer reports the first anywhere. v2 had it ("28\% of V1 neurons prefer $90^\circ$, 32\% of AL neurons $0^\circ$"); v3 has only the volume-wide distribution in Appendix B ("19\% of labelled neurons within $11^\circ$ of $0^\circ$, 25\% of $90^\circ$"). So the claim in the abstract and in the Discussion currently rests on numbers that were deleted along with the observation they supported.

Worse, the numbers that *are* reported contradict it for AL. Section 3.2: the factor "peaks at $90^\circ$ in V1 on both substrates ..., at $0^\circ$ in AL on the twin (1.49) and at $135^\circ$ in AL in vivo (1.69)". Methods states that "The orientation labels are in-vivo measurements on both substrates", so AL's dominant orientation is one fixed number and cannot be both 0° and 135°. At most one of the two AL rows is consistent with the claim, and the paper does not say which, or remark on the discrepancy at all. A 135° shift in the anchor between a substrate and the model fitted to it is not a rounding detail: it is direct evidence that the anisotropy is partly a property of the twin rather than of the circuit, which matters because the twin is where the mechanism's cleanest numbers come from.

What would resolve it: (i) report the per-area label distribution (mode and the fraction in the modal bin) for V1, RL, AL and LM, in the same sentence as the anisotropy peaks, so the reader can check the coincidence area by area; (ii) state which areas the coincidence holds for and which it does not, and if it is V1 only, say so and weaken the abstract and the Discussion to what is measured — the anisotropy is orientation-specific and area-specific, peaks at 90° in V1 on both substrates, and coincides with V1's dominant orientation; (iii) explain the AL twin/in-vivo disagreement, or report it as a limit alongside "The twin substrate is a model fitted to the same recordings". Also report the *volume-wide* anisotropy peak on both substrates, since the decoder is trained on populations that mix areas and it is the volume-wide residual, not any area's, that the decoder actually sees.

**3.2. The section's roadmap sentence asserts the decomposition the section then explicitly disavows, and no experiment in the paper separates circulant from residual in the decoder's input.**

Section 3.2, immediately after the symmetry paragraph:

> "Four observations locate the structure in the circulant part and narrow down what fixes the frame."

The second of those four observations says the opposite, correctly:

> "This does not show that the circulant part alone carries the structure, since the residual is still in that decoder's input."

Both sentences are on the same page. The correction of the random-rotation inference is exactly what v2 asked for and the new wording of the observation itself is right; the roadmap sentence then re-asserts the withdrawn inference as the section's thesis, and it is the sentence a skimming reader will take away. The Discussion inherits the same structure — "the relations fix the class structure up to the symmetries of the content, and a departure from that symmetry fixes the frame" — which is fine as a statement about what *can* fix the frame but is presented as "The symmetry analysis gives the mechanism".

The authors declined the leak-free projection ablation (first-stage class assignments from the training half, project test Gram rows onto the orthogonal complement of the training-half residual basis). That is a defensible call given how the last ablation went, but it means the circulant/residual division of labour — circulant carries the ordering, residual carries the frame — is a hypothesis the paper's experiments are consistent with, not one they demonstrate. The paper should say that in the roadmap sentence rather than in a subordinate clause three paragraphs later.

What would resolve it: reword to "Four observations narrow down what fixes the frame; a fifth points to a specific anisotropy, which a model population then tests", dropping "locate the structure in the circulant part"; and add one sentence to the limits saying that no experiment removes the residual from the input, so the assignment of ordering to the circulant part and frame to the residual is inferred from the symmetry argument and the model, not measured. If the authors reconsider the projection ablation later, the payoff is large — it is the one experiment that would convert this section from exclusion to demonstration.

**3.3. The residual is described as bi-cardinal, but a bi-cardinal residual cannot break the 0°/90° symmetry the decoder demonstrably breaks; the model that works is uni-modal, and the two are never reconciled.**

Section 3.2, first paragraph, on the residual's form:

> "Its form is a cardinal anisotropy, stronger relations among neurons near the cardinal axes, whose shape differs between areas".

Appendix B, from the decoder's side:

> "The relations say how close a neuron is to a cardinal axis, the anisotropy of Section~3.2 seen from the decoder's side, and much less about which oblique it prefers."

A structure that is "stronger among neurons near the cardinal axes", plural, is invariant under the 90° rotation that maps 0° onto 90°. It would leave the frame fixed only up to that rotation, and a decoder reading it could not tell horizontal from vertical. But the decoder does: "it is right for 87\% of all neurons on the twin and 79\% in vivo". So the operative residual must be *uni-modal* in the orientation class — which is what the anisotropy factor of the fifth observation measures ("at the class where it peaks", peaking at 90° in V1) and what the model implants ("a weight peaked at $90^\circ$"). The first paragraph's description and the fifth observation's measurement are descriptions of two different structures, and the paper never says which one it means, nor which one the 19%/29% residual variance is made of.

This matters beyond wording, because it is the hinge of the sufficiency argument. The model was matched to cortex on one scalar, a peak-over-mean ratio. Two residuals with the same factor but different shapes — one uni-modal at 90°, one with peaks at both 0° and 90° — have different symmetry-breaking power, and only the first can do what the paper needs.

What would resolve it, cheaply, from quantities already computed: (i) plot or tabulate the class-Gram residual as a function of orientation class for V1 in vivo and on the twin, and say in words whether it has one peak or two; (ii) report, for the model at each of the four strengths, the residual's fraction of class-Gram variance and compare it with the 19% (twin) and 29% (in vivo) measured in cortex — v2 asked for this and the factor was substituted for it, but the variance fraction is the quantity the symmetry argument is stated in; (iii) report the correlation between the model residual and the cortical residual, the same statistic already used for V1–RL (0.77) and V1–AL (0.31). If the model residual correlates with the cortical one at something like the V1–RL level, the sufficiency argument is much stronger than a matched scalar can make it; if it does not, the paper should say that the model reproduces the strength of the anisotropy, not its shape.

**3.4. The Allen single-animal result is stated against the 45° chance line, but against the paper's own best-constant baseline the "consistent signal" is 0.8° to 1.8°, and there is no null.**

The abstract: "Populations drawn from one animal carry only a weak signal, $2^\circ$ to $3^\circ$ below chance." Section 3.3: "Single-animal populations carry a weak but consistent signal, $1.9^\circ$ to $2.9^\circ$ below chance in all six runs, whether or not the animal was seen in training."

Table 2's own caption gives the right null: "Chance $45^\circ$, best constant given the Allen label distribution $43.9^\circ$". The six single-animal values are 42.5, 42.9, 42.1, 43.1, 43.1, 42.7. Against 43.9° those are 1.4, 1.0, 1.8, 0.8, 0.8 and 1.2 degrees — roughly half the stated margin, and three of the six sit within one degree of what a decoder that had learned nothing but the Allen label distribution would score. Fig. 5 draws only the 45° line, so the figure a reader forms an impression from shows the larger gap; Methods claims "Both lines are drawn in the figures", which is not true of Fig. 5 (nor of Fig. 2 or Fig. 3; see minor 1).

I accept that the authors declined a permutation null for these cells, and I would not insist on one if the margin were the stated 2–3°. At 0.8–1.8° it is no longer obvious by inspection, and "consistent" is carrying the argument: six runs in the same direction is the whole evidence, and the six are not independent (the same three animal splits appear in both rows, and the two rows share training animals). This is load-bearing: the contrast between single-animal and mixed populations is the paper's third headline claim, and the subsequent training-diversity explanation only has something to explain if the single-animal signal is real but weak.

What would resolve it, in order of cost: (i) state both margins wherever the claim appears, e.g. "$1.9^\circ$ to $2.9^\circ$ below the $45^\circ$ chance line and $0.8^\circ$ to $1.8^\circ$ below the best constant", and add the 43.9° line to Fig. 5; (ii) give a sign test or a paired comparison across the three splits, which costs nothing and is more honest than "consistent"; (iii) if a null is ever run, the cheapest form is to shuffle orientation labels within each test animal and rerun scoring on the saved predictions — no retraining, and it directly answers whether 42.5° differs from what the label distribution alone gives on that split.

Note also that the single-animal column uses 128-neuron populations and the mixed column 256, so the headline contrast in Table 2 is not matched on population size. The matched number exists (mixed populations of 128 score 36.0°, in the controls table) and rescues the comparison, but Table 2 should point at it.

## 4. Minor issues

1. **Methods claims baselines are in the figures and they are not, and one caption describes a line that does not exist.** Methods: "Both lines are drawn in the figures." Fig. 2's caption offers only "grey line: the $45^\circ$ error of an uninformed decoder", and the rendered figure has a single grey line. Fig. 5 likewise shows only "chance" and "label grid". Fig. 3's caption says "grey line: $45^\circ$ chance; dotted: best constant", but the rendered figure has one solid grey line at 45° and no best-constant line; the only dashed series in the panel is the red "fraction of predictions", which a reader will take for the dotted line the caption promises. Either draw the 41.3°/43.9° lines in Figs. 2, 3 and 5, or delete the Methods sentence and fix Fig. 3's caption. This is the third round in which the figures show only the 45° null.

2. **"An animal has about 280 labelled cells" contradicts Methods, and the sentence uses two meanings of "cell".** Methods: "33 mice, 9,281 cells, of which 5,529 carry an orientation label" — 5,529/33 ≈ 167 labelled cells per mouse. The 280 figure is *all* cells per mouse (9,281/33 = 281; equivalently 6,706 training cells over 24 training mice = 279). So the sentence should read "about 280 cells, about 170 of them labelled, half of them training cells". Then state explicitly whether "trained on about 140 cells per animal", the "280 or 560 training cells" pools, and the matched MICrONS run's "140 neurons of one scan" count all cells or only labelled ones. Open since v0.3, and it is the arithmetic behind the abstract's "That reflects the training diversity one animal provides, not its circuit."

3. **The Allen balanced/raw pair still does not match Table 2.** Appendix B: "the balanced error is $36.4^\circ$ against $35.7^\circ$ raw on the same saved predictions (held-out-animal split 0)". Table 2's held-out mixed row is "$35.2^\circ$ / $37.0^\circ$ / $38.4^\circ$", so split 0 is 35.2°, not 35.7°. Naming the run was the v2 request and it has been done; the number still disagrees. Either the split index or the value is wrong.

4. **The "85% of the gain" figure matches neither baseline.** Appendix B: "On orientation the relations retain about 85\% of the gain over chance that the aligned activity gives". Against 45°: (45 − 25.4)/(45 − 21.5) = 83% in vivo and (45 − 20.2)/(45 − 15.0) = 83% on the twin. Against the paper's preferred best constant of 41.3°: 80% and 80%. Write "about 80% of the gain over the best constant", since that is the null the paper argues for everywhere else. (The receptive-field "about 60\%" is right: 0.234/0.37 = 63%, 0.480/0.80 = 60%.)

5. **The oblique error is quoted three different ways.** Section 3.1: "within $15^\circ$ of an oblique, $36^\circ$ to $39^\circ$". Section 3.2: "$37^\circ$ to $40^\circ$ at the obliques on the twin". Appendix B: "oblique ones to $37^\circ$ to $45^\circ$". Fig. 3's twin bars read 36.9° at 45° and 39.4° at 135°, so the twin range is 37–39; Appendix B's upper end of 45 comes from the Allen 120°/150° bins and should be labelled as such. Pick one convention and use it in all three places.

6. **Appendix B still lumps the Allen cardinal bins.** "neurons preferring $0^\circ$ or $90^\circ$ are decoded to $11^\circ$ to $12^\circ$ on the twin and $25^\circ$ on Allen" — Section 3.3 already gives the right split ("cells labelled $0^\circ$ are decoded to $25^\circ$ and those labelled $90^\circ$ to $34^\circ$"), and Fig. 3's Allen panel shows 25 and 34. Third round; fix the appendix sentence.

7. **56% appears twice in one sentence with two different meanings.** "it is right for 87\% of all neurons on the twin and 79\% in vivo (majority answer 56\%; averaged over the eight true-orientation bins, 83\% and 74\%), and for the 56\% of neurons whose preference lies within $15^\circ$ of an axis, for 94\% and 86\%". The first is a majority-class accuracy, the second a fraction of the population. Third round; rephrase one.

8. **Section 3.4 misquotes its own appendix.** "from $0.21$ to $-0.25$ for mixed ones" — Appendix F's mixed rows are 0.22, 0.08, −0.25.

9. **The abstract and Fig. 5's caption round outward where Section 3.3 rounds correctly.** Section 3.3 now says "$1.9^\circ$ to $2.9^\circ$" and "$6.6^\circ$ to $9.8^\circ$"; the abstract still says "$2^\circ$ to $3^\circ$" and Fig. 5's caption "$2^\circ$ to $3^\circ$" and "$7^\circ$ to $10^\circ$", both wider than the data at both ends.

10. **The Discussion says "single animal" where it means "single scan".** "From the within-circuit correlations of a single animal it is readable only weakly, at $31^\circ$ to $33^\circ$ on MICrONS". MICrONS is one animal throughout, and the paper's headline number from that one animal is 25.4°. The 31–33° figures are the within-*scan* control. As written the sentence contradicts Section 3.1. (The abstract gets this right: "single-scan populations in MICrONS are decoded to $31^\circ$ to $33^\circ$" — though 32.0 ± 1.1 and 31.1 ± 1.2 would be more accurately quoted as 31° to 32°.)

11. **The abstract's causal connective points the wrong way.** "The readout is possible because the relations between orientation classes are close to circulant, which fixes the class structure up to rotation and reflection. The absolute frame comes from a departure from that symmetry." The circulant structure is precisely what makes the absolute readout *impossible*; the next sentence says so. Split it: the ordering is possible because the class relations are nearly circulant; the absolute frame is possible only because of a departure from that symmetry.

12. **"Frames" is overloaded.** Section 3.2: "an excess of cardinal frames in the stimulus does in three of five" — these are movie frames, in a section whose central term is the reference frame. Write "an excess of cardinal orientations among the stimulus frames".

13. **The class-balanced cross-area rows are in the controls table and never mentioned in the text.** "same, class-balanced training (2 seeds), V1 / RL / AL, raw & twin & $31^\circ$ / $29^\circ$ / $45^\circ$" with "\quad aligned & twin & $31^\circ$ / $29^\circ$ / $36^\circ$". Two things a reader will notice: V1→AL under class balance shows no transfer at all (45° raw, 36° aligned), and V1→V1 costs 9° relative to the unbalanced 22.4°, whereas whole-volume class balancing costs only 1.8° (20.2° → 22.0°). "no claim rests on these rows" covers the first; the 9° gap deserves a sentence, or the rows should be dropped.

14. **The V1→RL row has no population size.** The V1 and AL rows say "(128 neurons)"; "trained on V1, scored on RL, raw / aligned (3 seeds each)" does not. v2 asked for the population size in every row.

15. **The controls table's caption conflicts with two of its rows.** "single runs unless a seed count is given", but the "standard decoder" rows at 20.2° and 25.4° are the three-seed means of Table 1 (20.2 ± 0.4, 25.4 ± 0.5). Annotate them.

16. **The fourth-seed R² is a two-sigma outlier and is reported to two decimals.** Appendix B: "on a fourth seed of the Table~\ref{tab:microns} configuration, run with saved predictions, $\Rsq$ is 0.50 absolute and 0.50 within scan on the twin", against Table 1's 0.480 ± 0.010 over three seeds. Attributing it to a fourth seed was the v2 request and closes the bookkeeping question; give the value to three decimals so the reader can see whether it is 0.495 or 0.504.

17. **The complementary ablation is still missing, and it matters more now than it did in v2.** Section 3.2 now uses the row-statistics result as evidence — "The three row statistics alone ... fix a weak frame by themselves ($37.7^\circ$ raw and aligned)". The complement, the Gram row with the three statistics removed, is the one number that says how much of the frame is in the per-neuron scalars and how much in the relations proper. It is one training run.

18. **Two TODOs remain.** "TODO: affiliation and co-authors" and "\paragraph{Code and data.} TODO: repository URL." The second is a hard requirement at both target venues.

19. **A hard-coded cross-reference.** Methods: "so every receptive-field result, in vivo included, scores relations against model-derived positions (Section~4)". Use `\ref`.

20. **Two pieces of prose still perform rather than state.** "This says what a stimulus-aligned readout can be applied to, not that it lost a contest" — the contest metaphor adds nothing; v2's own phrasing ("not a comparison the activity decoder could have won") was closer, and plainer still would be "this shows which inputs each readout can be applied to, not which readout is better". And the section title "What moves the numbers" is a nudge where "Decoder size, population size and training" would tell the reader what is in the section.

21. **Fig. 1's caption is about 250 words and restates the Protocol paragraph of Methods almost in full.** Cut it to the panel descriptions and point at Methods for the split/sample distinction.

**On length.** 21 pages is one or two too many, and the removable material is all in the back. I would cut, in order: Appendix C (classification results) and Appendix D (labelled reference models), about 1.5 pages with their tables — the paper itself says the first "lead[s] to the same conclusions" on a scale that "counts a near miss as a miss" and that the second "is one linear labelled model, not a ceiling ... not comparable with the angular errors of the main text", so both are footnotes rather than appendices; Fig. A3, a full page supporting the single sentence "Every model above 2M memorises its training neurons within 10 to 20 epochs"; and Fig. 4, the four-regime schematic, which duplicates the Methods paragraph and Table 2's own row/column structure and would work as a small inset in Fig. 5. Section 3.5 ("What moves the numbers") is a sweep summary that belongs with Appendix A, leaving two sentences in the Discussion. That is about three pages, taking the paper to 18, and none of it costs an argument.

The residual-variance-fraction comparison v2 also asked for was replaced by the factor rather than added; see my 3.3.
- **3.2 (V1→AL rotation equally predicted by the two areas' label priors)** — **resolved, by withdrawal.** The 78° was a single-seed artefact, the observation is gone from Section 3.2, the rows stay in the controls table with all seeds, and the class-balanced rerun v2 asked for was run. Residues: the class-balanced rows are uninterpreted (minor 13), and the per-area claim the observation used to support survives in the abstract with its supporting counts deleted (my 3.1).
- **3.3 ("the circulant part carries the whole per-neuron structure" does not follow)** — **partly resolved.** The observation itself is reworded correctly and the caveat is stated explicitly; the section's roadmap sentence still asserts the withdrawn inference, and the leak-free projection ablation was declined, so the decomposition remains inferred (my 3.2).
- **3.4 (thresholded frame-fixed counts, text/table contradiction, unexplained failing seeds)** — **resolved.** Text now matches the table ("fix the frame in at most one seed of five"), threshold sensitivity at 25° and 35° is reported, and the degenerate run is identified. - **3.5 (leaking ablation disclosed in one subordinate clause)** — **resolved.** Appendix B now has a dedicated paragraph with the construction, both numbers (20.7° at 8 classes, 17.7° at 24), the diagnostic control (17.2°/15.6°) and the explanation of why a true-class offset is label injection, plus a sentence in the limits.
- **3.6 (cardinal-only readout hidden behind a mean angular error)** — **resolved.** Fig. 3 promoted to the main text, per-band errors in Section 3.1, bin-averaged coarse accuracies added (83%/74%, 61% on Allen), Discussion opener and limits reworded. Seven of those nine are one-line text fixes.

## 6. Recommendation

**Minor revision.**

v3 answered seven of v2's eight major concerns with real work rather than rewording, and withdrew a claim when the seeds did not support it, which is the behaviour that makes the rest of the paper credible; what remains is one substantive gap — the per-area "dominant orientation" coincidence is asserted in the abstract with the supporting counts deleted and contradicted by the paper's own AL numbers — plus a set of text and figure fixes, two of which (the residual's shape, the Allen best-constant comparison) need nothing but quantities already computed. I would ask for concerns 3.1 through 3.4 and minors 1–10 before acceptance and would not need to see the paper a fourth time.

**What a hostile reviewer 2 would say.** Four points, all defensible. (i) Every MICrONS claim comes from one animal whose training and test halves share recording sessions, and the paper concedes it; the multi-animal dataset produces a result that exists only for a designed 40-condition grating set, which is a tuning-profile similarity matrix and therefore closer to classical RSA than to the paper's "discard the stimulus" premise, and it vanishes on natural movies. (ii) The decoder is functionally a cardinal-axis classifier with a graded distance readout — obliques are at chance on both datasets — so "determines each neuron's preferred orientation" oversells a 25° mean. (iii) The mechanism section has now lost the only causal-looking observation it had: the direct ablation leaked labels and was withdrawn, the cross-area rotation was a single-seed artefact and was withdrawn, and what remains is an exclusion argument in n = 1 animal plus a synthetic model matched on one scalar — a hypothesis, not a result, and reviewer 2 will say the honest place for it is a paragraph in the Discussion rather than a headline in the abstract. (iv) On Allen, single-animal populations beat the paper's own best constant by 0.8° to 1.8° with no null, which reviewer 2 will read as "the single-animal cells are at chance and the paper says otherwise" (my 3.4). My own view is that (i) and (ii) are already conceded in the limits and should also be named in the Discussion's opening rather than only in its closing paragraph; (iii) is answerable by reframing Section 3.2 as a constraint plus a sufficiency demonstration, which is what it now is; and (iv) is answerable in one sentence and a line on a figure.
