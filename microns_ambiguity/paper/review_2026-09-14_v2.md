# Review: "What the correlations between neurons say about each neuron" (Draft v2)

## 1. Summary

The paper asks whether the pairwise correlation matrix of a sampled neural population, with the stimulus discarded and no neuron labelled at test time, determines each neuron's preferred orientation and receptive-field position. A transformer takes the standardised correlation matrix of a sampled population, one row per neuron plus three row statistics, and emits a per-neuron orientation vector or an (x, y) position. On MICrONS (one mouse, 13 scans, 12,894 co-registered neurons) it reaches 25.4° mean angular error in vivo and 20.2° on the digital twin, against 45° for an uninformed decoder and 41.3° for the best constant guess, with R² = 0.23 and 0.48 for receptive fields; the coarse readout is that the decoder names the correct cardinal axis for 79% of in-vivo neurons against a 56% majority, and does not resolve the obliques at all. The proposed mechanism is that the class-averaged correlation matrix is nearly circulant, which fixes the orientation classes only up to the dihedral group D₈, and that a residual cardinal anisotropy (29% of class-Gram variance in vivo, 19% on the twin) supplies the absolute frame; the residual is shown to survive class balancing, disjoint stimulus halves and neuron-half resampling, to differ between areas, and to track each area's over-represented orientation through stronger co-fluctuation among like-tuned neurons. Across 33 Allen mice, with relations from drifting gratings only, orientation transfers to held-out animals (≈36°) when populations mix animals and is 1.9–2.9° below chance when they do not, which the paper attributes to training diversity rather than to the circuit and supports with a matched single-scan MICrONS run. Since v0.3 the substantive additions are circular regression throughout, activity-seeing reference decoders, a class-balanced population control, the V1→AL transfer with its measured rotation angle, the matched single-scan training run, a five-seed synthetic block with strength sweeps, and a new synthetic switch implanting shared variability among like-tuned neurons peaked at one orientation.

## 2. Three strongest aspects

1. **The control battery is now close to exemplary, and several of the new controls are the ones a sceptic would actually ask for.** The stimulus-known reference decoders (Table 3) are the right way to put a scale on what the Gram discards, and the finding that a *linear* readout of a neuron's own responses matches the activity transformer ("so the activity decoder is in effect a per-neuron tuning readout, and what it has over the relational decoder is the alignment of the responses to the stimulus") is self-effacing and correct. The matched single-scan MICrONS run ("a decoder trained on 140 neurons of one scan and tested on the other scans scores $38^\circ$ to $44^\circ$ on the twin") converts the Allen training-diversity argument from indirect to direct, exactly as the previous round asked. The Allen stimulus-known reference on single-animal populations ("reaches $33^\circ$ to $35^\circ$, the same as with mixed populations") cleanly separates "the neurons don't carry it" from "the decoder can't learn it".

2. **The random-rotation training experiment remains the single best idea in the paper, and it is now paired with a second one.** Training with each population's targets rotated at random and the loss taken after the best alignment, then observing that the decoder still anchors a consistent frame on the data ("That decoder's raw error, $25^\circ$ and $31^\circ$ against $45^\circ$ for an arbitrary frame, shows it anchors a consistent frame on the data even when nothing in the loss asks for one"), is a genuine answer to the "the frame lives in the weights" objection. The class-balanced-population control ("With every training and test population drawn with equal numbers of neurons from each of the eight orientation classes ... $22.0^\circ$ on the twin and $26.6^\circ$ in vivo ... with no gap between raw and aligned error") is the second, and together they close off the two most obvious deflationary readings.

3. **The paper has become markedly more honest since v0.3, in ways that cost it headline numbers.** The abstract now carries three baselines instead of one, states the Allen grating dependence, and says outright "what it does not resolve is the obliques". Section 3.3 opens with the natural-movie failure rather than burying it. Methods now state that "every receptive-field result, in vivo included, scores relations against model-derived positions". The synthetic table reports ranges over five seeds and an explicit count of seeds in which the frame was fixed, rather than quoting the agreeing cells. The failed ablation is disclosed rather than quietly dropped. This is the kind of revision that makes a reviewer trust the rest of the paper, and it should be said plainly.

## 3. Major concerns

**3.1. At the anisotropy strength actually measured in cortex, the synthetic model fixes the frame in one of two seeds, and the abstract states the result unconditionally.**

The abstract asserts: "the neurons that prefer it co-fluctuate most strongly, and a model population with only that property recovers the absolute frame." The Discussion repeats it: "a model population with that co-fluctuation and nothing else recovers the absolute frame, while models with sharper or more numerous cardinal neurons do not." But the only synthetic cell tuned to the measured cortical strength is Table 7's

> `\quad $\times 1.2$ (two seeds) & $31.5^\circ$ [15--48] & $24.2^\circ$ [15--33] & 1 / 2`

and Section 3.2 concedes it in passing: "in one of two seeds at 1.2-fold, the factor of the twin V1 data". A mean raw error of 31.5° is barely better than the *aligned* error of the circulant baseline (30.0°), i.e. at the strength the data actually show, the model on average does not fix a frame. The cells that do work (×1.55 at 4/5, ×2.1 at 2/2) are at anisotropies stronger than the one being modelled. Two seeds is also not a sample.

Two things would resolve this, and one of them may well work in the authors' favour. First, the "factor" is never defined. From Section 3.2's own numbers, if the factor is the peak same-class correlation over the mean of the others, twin V1 gives 0.071/≈0.055 ≈ 1.3, **in vivo V1 gives 0.095/≈0.064 ≈ 1.5, and AL gives 0.204/≈0.125 ≈ 1.6**. The paper matched its "data factor" to the *twin*, the weaker substrate, and in doing so understated its own result: the in-vivo factor appears to land almost exactly on the ×1.55 row, which fixes the frame in four of five seeds. Define the factor in Methods, report it for in-vivo V1, in-vivo AL and twin AL as well as twin V1, and match the synthetic strength to the in-vivo value. Second, run 10 seeds at the data-matched strength (and at 1.2 and 1.35) and report the fraction, not a two-seed anecdote. Third, and most informative: report for each synthetic condition the *residual fraction of class-Gram variance* and show that the frame is fixed once it crosses the 19–29% observed in cortex. That is the quantity the mechanism argument is actually about, and it would turn Table 7 from a switch list into a dose-response curve.

Until then, the abstract should read something like "a model population with only that property recovers the absolute frame once the anisotropy is at or above the strength measured in vivo", and the Discussion sentence should carry the same qualifier.

**3.2. The V1→AL rotation is equally predicted by the two areas' label priors, and the paper's own sentence cites the counts as its evidence.**

Section 3.2's fifth observation is the closest thing in the paper to a causal test of the anchor:

> "a decoder trained on V1 transfers to RL ... and to AL, whose anisotropy differs, with a raw error of $58^\circ$ that falls to $32^\circ$ after one global rotation of $78^\circ$: it carries the structure and the wrong frame, and the rotation it needs is close to the $90^\circ$ that separates the two areas' dominant orientations (28\% of V1 neurons prefer $90^\circ$, 32\% of AL neurons $0^\circ$)."

The evidence offered for the 78° is a comparison with the *counts* — the fraction of neurons preferring each orientation — which is precisely the explanation the paper rules out everywhere else ("the frame is not the uneven number of neurons per orientation", "the signature is in the correlations, not in the counts"). And there is a trivial alternative that produces the same 78° with no relational mechanism at all: a decoder trained on V1 inherits V1's label prior and predicts 90° disproportionately (Fig. A5 shows 63–72% of predictions falling on the two cardinal axes); applied to AL, whose labels concentrate at 0°, the best single global rotation will be whatever moves the prediction distribution onto the target distribution, i.e. ≈90°. The observed 58°→32° drop shows *structure* transfers, which is real and worth reporting; it does not show that the *frame* is anchored by the correlational anisotropy rather than by the training prior.

Three things would separate the two accounts, all cheap:
- Report the rotation between the V1 and AL **class-Gram residuals** (their principal phase), and compare it with 78°. This was requested in the previous round and is still not reported; the paper compares against the label mode instead. If the residual phase difference is near 78°, the mechanism is nailed; if it is near 90° but the label difference is also 90°, say so and concede the confound is not separable from this pair alone.
- Rerun V1→AL with **class-balanced training and test populations** (the machinery already exists: "class-balanced populations (64 per class)"). Balancing removes the prior; if the required rotation survives, the count explanation is dead.
- Report the rotation angle for V1→RL, not only the "$1.6^\circ$ frame gap", together with the V1–RL residual phase difference, so the reader has two points and not one.

Note also that all three transfer rows in Table 6 are single unlabelled runs (see 3.8).

**3.3. "The circulant part carries the whole per-neuron structure" does not follow from the random-rotation experiment.**

Section 3.2, second observation:

> "a decoder trained with no frame in its labels, each population's targets rotated at random and the loss taken after the best alignment of predictions to targets, places neurons to $19.9^\circ$ on the twin and $25.5^\circ$ in vivo after a single global alignment, the same as the standard decoder with the frame ($20.2^\circ$, $25.4^\circ$): the circulant part carries the whole per-neuron structure, and the frame comes at no cost."

The inference is invalid as stated, and it was flagged in the previous round in the same words. Randomising the *labels'* frame does not remove the residual from the *input*: the random-rotation decoder's Gram still contains the full anisotropy, and nothing prevents it from using the residual to compute the per-neuron ordering. What the experiment establishes is that the per-neuron structure is recoverable without any frame in the supervision, and that a frame is anchored anyway — both valuable, neither the stated conclusion.

Resolution: reword to "the per-neuron structure is recoverable with no frame anywhere in the labels, and adding the frame to the labels costs nothing", which is what was measured. If the authors want the stronger claim, they need a decoder whose *input* is circulant-projected. A leak-free construction: estimate the class-Gram residual basis on the training half only, obtain test-neuron class assignments from a first-stage decoder trained on the training half (never from test labels), and project each test Gram row onto the orthogonal complement of that basis. If the aligned error survives while the raw error goes to 45°, the claim is demonstrated.

**3.4. The synthetic table's summary is a thresholded count, the text contradicts the table in one place, and the failing seeds are never explained.**

The "frame fixed" column is defined in the caption as "raw error below $30^\circ$ and within $5^\circ$ of the aligned error". Two arbitrary thresholds, and the counts they produce are the numbers the text quotes ("four of five seeds", "in one of two"). With ranges like "$23.5^\circ$ [4--54]" and "$18.4^\circ$ [3--46]" the mean carries almost no information and the count is the whole summary, so its threshold sensitivity matters. Report the per-seed raw and aligned errors in full (a strip plot or a wide table), and state how the counts move if the criterion is 25° or 35°.

The text also contradicts the table. Appendix B: "Sharper cardinal tuning and a cardinal excess of neurons do not fix the frame, alone, together, or at any strength"; Section 3.2: "Sharper cardinal tuning and a cardinal excess of neurons do not fix the frame at any strength tested". But Table 7 gives

> `cardinal excess of neurons (count) & $37.8^\circ$ [27--45] & $35.1^\circ$ [27--42] & 1 / 5`
> `count + sharper tuning & $42.8^\circ$ [29--58] & $35.2^\circ$ [27--41] & 1 / 5`

so the count manipulation fixed the frame in one seed of five in each of two conditions. Change "do not fix the frame" to "fix it in at most one of five seeds". (The strength sweep is reported as "two seeds each, all above $42^\circ$ raw", which is also in mild tension with the base-strength range starting at 27°.)

Finally, the "all three" row has a seed at raw ≈46° and **aligned ≈44°**, worse on the aligned metric than the "none (circulant)" baseline's mean of 30.0°: that run recovered neither frame nor ordering. The appendix's explanation — "The spread across seeds is large in every cell where the frame is not fixed, because selection on the raw error then has no signal to select on" — cannot cover a run whose aligned error is also at chance, since ordering is recoverable in the baseline condition. Report the training curves for those seeds and say whether they are optimisation failures; if they are, flag them, do not average them in silently.

**3.5. The leaking ablation is disclosed in a single subordinate clause, and a reader cannot reconstruct what happened or why it matters.**

The whole treatment is:

> "We could not isolate the anchor by subtraction: removing the class-level residual from every Gram with the true classes injects the classes into the Gram (a control that removes the whole class structure improves the score), so the frame is located by exclusion and by the cross-area pattern, not by a direct ablation of the candidate."

This is the right thing to say and I credit the authors for saying it. But it is also the single most important methodological event since v0.3 — an ablation that a previous draft presented as evidence turned out to leak labels — and the paper gives it one clause and no numbers. A reader cannot tell what the ablation was, what it reported, how the leak was detected, or that the paper's central causal claim now rests on exclusion *because* of it. That matters for calibration: exclusion arguments are weaker than ablations, and the reader should know the paper tried and failed rather than merely didn't try.

Resolution: an appendix paragraph of five or six sentences giving (i) the construction, (ii) the number it produced, (iii) the diagnostic that revealed the leak — the sentence "a control that removes the whole class structure improves the score" is the diagnostic and deserves to be stated as one, with its number, (iv) why offset subtraction with true classes is label injection. Add one sentence to the Discussion's limits paragraph. And consider the leak-free alternative sketched in 3.3.

**3.6. The orientation readout is essentially "which cardinal axis, and how far from it", but the headline number is a mean angular error that invites a continuous reading, and the oblique error never appears as a number in the main text.**

Fig. A5 shows the twin oblique bins at ≈37° and ≈39° and the in-vivo ones at ≈39° and ≈38.5°, against a 45° chance line; Appendix B states it, "oblique ones to $37^\circ$ to $45^\circ$ ... and 63 to 72\% of all predictions fall on the two cardinal axes ... The relations say how close a neuron is to a cardinal axis ... and much less about which oblique it prefers". On Allen, "the cells labelled $120^\circ$ or $150^\circ$ are decoded at chance". So the content recovered is roughly: which of two cardinal axes (79%/87% correct against 56%), plus a graded distance from that axis, plus nothing at the obliques.

The abstract does say "what it does not resolve is the obliques", which is to the authors' credit. But the Discussion's opening sentence does not: "The correlation matrix of a few hundred cortical neurons, with the stimulus unknown and no neuron labelled, determines each neuron's preferred orientation to a mean error of $25^\circ$ in vivo". A reader who stops at Table 1 will take 25.4° as a uniform per-neuron precision, which it is not.

Resolution: add a column or two rows to Table 1 giving the error restricted to neurons within 15° of a cardinal axis and the error restricted to neurons within 15° of an oblique (the latter is ≈38–40°, i.e. near chance). Promote Fig. A5 to the main text — it is the single most informative figure about what the readout actually is. Reword the Discussion's first sentence to name the cardinal structure. And give the *balanced* cardinal-axis accuracy: 79%/87% are raw accuracies against a raw 56% majority, and the previous round's request for balanced coarse readouts is still unmet even though the balanced angular error is now reported.

**3.7. The Discussion's "Three limits" paragraph omits the limits this draft's own results establish.**

The paragraph covers exactly three things: one animal with shared sessions, label noise and the 30° Allen grid, and the twin-derived receptive-field labels. It does not mention (i) that the entire Allen result exists only with grating relations and "do[es] not replicate on naturalistic relations" — a fact stated once, in the first clause of Section 3.3, and nowhere in the Discussion, even though the Discussion calls "the Allen held-out-animal cells ... the cleaner generalisation test"; (ii) that the obliques are not resolved; (iii) that the direct ablation of the candidate anchor failed and the mechanism is established by exclusion; (iv) that the synthetic support for the anchor is seed-sensitive at the measured strength (3.1). Each of these is already somewhere in the paper; a reader assembling the honest caveat list has to do it themselves.

Resolution: extend the paragraph to five or six limits. This costs the authors nothing they have not already conceded elsewhere, and it is the difference between a paper that reads as careful and one that reads as careful-where-convenient.

**3.8. The rows that carry Section 3.2 are single runs with no seed count, while the rows that carry Section 3.1 have three.**

Table 1 and Table 3's principal rows now give mean ± s.d. over three seeds, and Table 7 gives five seeds with ranges — real progress. But Table 6, the controls table, carries the mechanism, and its caption says nothing about seeds; the rows that are not explicitly annotated ("3 seeds", "2 seeds") are presumably single runs. Those include every row on which 3.2's fifth observation rests:

> `trained on V1, scored on V1, raw / aligned & twin & $22.2^\circ$ / $22.2^\circ$`
> `trained on V1, scored on RL, raw / aligned & twin & $26.2^\circ$ / $24.6^\circ$`
> `trained on V1, scored on AL, raw / aligned & twin & $58.2^\circ$ / $31.6^\circ$`
> `within-scan populations (128 neurons) & twin / in vivo & $30.7^\circ$ / $32.6^\circ$`
> `mixed-scan populations (128 neurons, V1) & twin & $22.2^\circ$`

Section 3.5 states "Split-to-split variance on held-out mice exceeds seed variance", which tells the reader that seed variance is not the dominant term on Allen but says nothing about MICrONS, where Table 1's s.d. is 0.4–0.5°. A 1.6° "frame gap" for V1→RL is reported as a positive finding with no error bar at all.

Resolution: three seeds and a spread for every row of Table 6, and a caption line stating the seed count for unannotated rows. Also add a summary statistic (mean and range) to Table 2, which currently lists three bare split values per cell and leaves the reader to average them; the text's summaries ("$7^\circ$ to $10^\circ$ below chance", "$2^\circ$ to $3^\circ$") are then unverifiable against the table without arithmetic (and are slightly off; see 4.5 and 4.6).

## 4. Minor issues

1. **The chance sentence in Methods is still wrong as written.** "A decoder that carries no information produces errors spread evenly over that range, whatever it outputs, so chance is $45^\circ$." This holds only if the output is independent of the label *and* one of the two marginals is uniform on the circle — which is exactly why the paper needs a best-constant baseline of 41.3°. Reword: "...if its output is independent of the label and either labels or outputs are uniform over the orientation circle; with the MICrONS label distribution the best constant prediction scores $41.3^\circ$ (Appendix B)."

2. **The Allen cell-count arithmetic does not close, and it is load-bearing for the diversity argument.** Methods: "a held-out-animal split has 6,706 training cells and 2,575 test cells in total"; Appendix F implies nine test mice ("over the 9 test mice"). That is ≈280 cells per mouse on either side. But Section 3.3 says "A single animal contributes about 140 training cells" and calls "a pool of only 280 or 560 training neurons" "the size of two to four animals". Either 140 counts only orientation-labelled cells (5,529/9,281 = 60% of 280 ≈ 167, still not 140) or the split is over 36 containers rather than 33 mice, or the pool-size control counts something different from the 6,706 it is compared against. State the number of training animals/containers per split, the cells per animal, and whether "training neurons" in the pool-size control means all cells or labelled cells. As written, one sentence uses two different meanings of "training neuron".

3. **The pool-size control's reference value appears in no table.** "against $36.0^\circ$ from the full pool of 6,706 at the same population size of 128" — Table 2's mixed held-out values are 35.2/37.0/38.4 at population size 256. Add the 128-neuron runs to a table.

4. **Receptive-field R² disagrees between Table 1 and Appendix B by more than the stated seed spread.** Table 1: "$0.480 \pm 0.010$" and "$0.234 \pm 0.007$". Appendix B, scan control: "$\Rsq$ is 0.50 absolute and 0.50 within scan on the twin, 0.25 and 0.25 in vivo". 0.50 and 0.25 both sit more than two s.d. above the Table 1 means, so they must be different runs or a different configuration. This was raised in the previous round and is unresolved. Say which run each belongs to, or harmonise.

5. **The Allen balanced/raw pair still matches nothing.** "the best constant is $43.9^\circ$, and the balanced error is $36.4^\circ$ against $35.7^\circ$ raw." 35.7° appears in neither row of Table 2 (held-out: 35.2/37.0/38.4, mean 36.9; training animals: 35.3/37.2/36.1, mean 36.2). Raised last round, still open. Name the run.

6. **Two summary ranges are slightly outside their data.** "Mixed populations are $7^\circ$ to $10^\circ$ below chance" — the six values give 6.6° to 9.8°. "$2^\circ$ to $3^\circ$ below chance in all six runs" — the six values give 1.9° to 2.9°. Both appear in the abstract, Section 3.3 and the Fig. 5 caption. Round down, not up.

7. **Two cost figures are rounded generously.** "Test Grams built from stimulus bins the decoder never trained on cost $1^\circ$ on the twin and $9^\circ$ in vivo": the actual differences are 20.7 − 20.2 = 0.5° and 33.8 − 25.4 = 8.4° (or 33.8 − 25.3 = 8.5° against the matched 60-bin control). Write "about $0.5^\circ$" and "about $8.5^\circ$", or say which comparison is intended.

8. **The "85%" retention figure is not stated against a baseline, and against the paper's own preferred baseline it is 80%.** "On orientation the relations retain about 85\% of the gain over chance that the aligned activity gives". Against 45°: (45−20.2)/(45−15.0) = 83% on the twin and (45−25.4)/(45−21.5) = 83% in vivo. Against the best constant 41.3°: 80% on both. Since the paper has gone to the trouble of introducing 41.3° as the honest null, use it here and write "about 80%".

9. **56% appears twice in one sentence with two different meanings.** "it is right for 87\% of all neurons on the twin and 79\% in vivo (chance 56\%), and for the 56\% of neurons whose preference lies within $15^\circ$ of an axis, for 94\% and 86\%." The first 56% is the majority-class accuracy, the second is a fraction of the neuron population. Rephrase one of them.

10. **Text and Fig. A5 still disagree on Allen.** "neurons preferring $0^\circ$ or $90^\circ$ are decoded to $11^\circ$ to $12^\circ$ on the twin and $25^\circ$ on Allen". In the Allen panel of Fig. A5 the 0° bar is ≈25° but the 90° bar is ≈34°. Give the two separately for Allen. (Raised last round; still open.) Also, for Allen the 120° and 150° bars sit slightly *above* the 45° line, so "oblique ones to $37^\circ$ to $45^\circ$" understates them; and calling the 30/60/120/150 bins "oblique" is loose on a 30° grid.

11. **Appendix A and Section 3.5 measure the dropped augmentations against different yardsticks.** Appendix A: "Neither changes the angular error by more than the seed spread (Section 3.5)." Section 3.5: "single runs, against a split-to-split spread of $3^\circ$". Fig. A4 is cited elsewhere as showing that split variance exceeds seed variance, so these are not interchangeable, and the quoted range (35.2 to 38.0, i.e. 2.8°) is at the edge of the 3° split spread either way. Harmonise the wording, and say which quantity the comparison uses.

12. **The Discussion reverts to the 45° null the abstract carefully qualified.** "against $45^\circ$ for an uninformed decoder" — add the 41.3° best constant, which is one clause. Likewise Fig. 2's "grey line: the $45^\circ$ error of an uninformed decoder" should gain a second line at 41.3°, since Fig. 2 is where most readers will form their impression of effect size.

13. **Table 2's caption gives the wrong null for Allen.** "Chance $45^\circ$; label grid $7.5^\circ$." Appendix B reports the Allen best constant as 43.9°. Add it to the caption.

14. **Two rows of Table 6 both read 22.2° at different population sizes and are easily confused.** "trained on V1, scored on V1, raw / aligned & twin & $22.2^\circ$ / $22.2^\circ$" (512-neuron populations) and "mixed-scan populations (128 neurons, V1) & twin & $22.2^\circ$". Put the population size in every row of the table.

15. **The within-scan / mixed-scan comparison is not fully matched, and its in-vivo leg is missing.** Section 3.3 compares "$30.7^\circ$ on the twin and $32.6^\circ$ in vivo (128 neurons)" against "$22^\circ$ for mixed-scan populations of the same size", but the table's mixed-scan row is restricted to V1 and has no in-vivo entry. State whether the within-scan row is also V1-restricted, and add the in-vivo mixed-scan number so the in-vivo comparison is checkable.

16. **The row-statistics control is stronger than reported and one number is missing.** The definition ("the mean, standard deviation and third moment of the row") and the confound check ("their correlations with the distance to the nearest cardinal axis are below 0.12 in magnitude and with gOSI below 0.16, and gOSI is 0.39 for cardinal and 0.37 for oblique neurons") resolve last round's concern well. But 37.7° from three scalars is 3.6° better than the best constant of 41.3°, so the scalars carry a little frame information on their own. Report the **frame-aligned** error of the row-statistics-only decoder: if raw ≈ aligned, per-neuron scalars anchor a frame, which belongs in Section 3.2. Also report the Gram row *without* the statistics, which is the complementary ablation and is not in the table.

17. **Say which substrate is which in the residual reproducibility figures.** "it correlates at 0.94 between random halves of the neurons, at 0.84 and 0.99 between disjoint halves of the stimulus bins" — presumably in vivo and twin, but the reader has to guess.

18. **Section 3.2 is a single ≈900-word paragraph carrying five numbered observations, a synthetic summary and a failed ablation.** This is the hardest part of the paper to read and the part most likely to be skimmed. Break it into subparagraphs with short lead sentences, and promote the random-rotation experiment and the class-balanced-population control out of the middle of the block — they are the two strongest pieces of evidence in the section and are currently buried.

19. **Two TODOs remain**: the author footnote ("TODO: affiliation and co-authors") and "Code and data. TODO: repository URL". Appendices E and F, which were TODOs last round, are now filled.

20. **Appendix D's numbers are not comparable to the main text and the caption does not say so loudly enough.** "from the classification-era runs (orientation as 8 or 6 classes, accuracy; receptive fields as $\Rsq$)" — the orientation column cannot be compared with any angular error in the paper, and "the relational decoder exceeded it on Allen receptive fields" is asserted with no number. Add the number, and state in the caption that the orientation column is not on the main text's scale. The same applies to Appendix C, whose Allen rows use "17M decoder with the condition-subset augmentation" while the final Allen configuration is 2.2M plain: say explicitly that Table 5 is not the same set of runs as Table 2.

21. **The four-class Allen bar is flagged but kept.** The Fig. 5 caption now says it "is ill-defined for labels on a $30^\circ$ grid and is shown only for comparison with MICrONS". If it is ill-defined, either drop it or state the tie-breaking rule for a 30° label that is equidistant from two of the four classes.

22. **Missing citations on the two facts the mechanism rests on.** The paper asserts a cardinal over-representation in mouse visual cortex ("19\% of labelled neurons within $11^\circ$ of $0^\circ$, 25\% of $90^\circ$") and an area-dependent dominant orientation, and then builds its synthetic anchor out of shared variability among like-tuned neurons — both are established literatures (cardinal/oblique bias and area-specific orientation preference in mouse visual areas; tuning-similarity dependence of noise correlations) and neither is cited. Adding two or three references here would show the anisotropy is not an artefact of this dataset, which is exactly what the mechanism argument needs.

23. **The abstract's last sentence is a methods observation.** "Capacity helps where labels are plentiful and stops helping where they are scarce." True and useful in Section 3.5; as the closing line of the abstract it reads as a non sequitur after the cross-animal result. Either drop it or end on the receptive-field finding.

24. **The abstract is asymmetric about the balanced metric**: it gives "$28^\circ$ when every orientation is weighted equally" for in vivo but only the raw 20° for the twin (balanced 24.5°). Give both or neither.

25. **Say which split the coarse readouts come from.** Section 3.1's "66\% (twin) and 59\% (in vivo)" and the 87/79% figures should be annotated with the neuron split, since Fig. 2's caption says the panel shows "neuron splits 1 and 2" and Table 1's balanced column is explicitly "(neuron split 2)".

26. **The in-vivo receptive-field caveat does not reach the Discussion or the abstract.** Methods now say it plainly ("every receptive-field result, in vivo included, scores relations against model-derived positions"), but the Discussion still singles out the twin case ("the twin receptive-field result compares a model's correlations with the same model's receptive fields, while the orientation labels are in-vivo measurements throughout") and the abstract reports "$\Rsq = 0.23$ ... from in-vivo responses in the MICrONS volume" with no qualifier. One clause in each place.

## 5. Status of the v0.3 major concerns

- **3.1 (synthetic table contradicts its conclusion; only agreeing rows quoted)** — **mostly resolved.** Five seeds, ranges, an explicit frame-fixed count, epoch now selected on the raw error, and the two problem cells have moved (count+stimulus 3/5, all three 4/5). ## 6. Recommendation

**Major revision, at the light end** — closer to a strong minor revision than to a resubmission.

The empirical core (Sections 3.1, 3.3, 3.4, and Appendix B) is solid, the protocol is airtight, and the revision has answered most of the previous round's substantive requests with real experiments rather than rewording; the four changes I would insist on before acceptance (seed replication of Table 6, a class-balanced or residual-phase check on the V1→AL rotation, ten seeds at the data-matched synthetic strength with the "factor" defined, and a corrected Discussion limits paragraph) are all reruns of existing pipelines plus text. What keeps it out of minor revision is that the paper's second headline claim — that a specific correlational anisotropy fixes the absolute frame — is still established by exclusion plus a synthetic sufficiency demo that works in one of two seeds at the strength the data show, while the abstract states it without qualification.

**What reviewer 2 will probably say.** I expect a split. A sympathetic reviewer 2 will recommend minor revision and focus on presentation: Section 3.2 is an unreadable wall, promote Fig. A5, fix the baselines in the figures. A hostile reviewer 2 will write a short, hard review around three points, and all three are defensible: (i) *n* = 1 animal for every MICrONS claim, and the paper's own Discussion concedes that "MICrONS is one animal, and its training and test halves share recording sessions"; (ii) the decoder is, functionally, a cardinal-axis classifier — obliques are at chance on both datasets — so "determines each neuron's preferred orientation" overstates what a 25° mean error means; (iii) the Allen cross-animal result exists only for a designed 40-condition grating set, which is a tuning-profile similarity matrix and therefore much closer to classical RSA than to the paper's "discard the stimulus" premise, and it vanishes on natural movies. I would also expect reviewer 2 to be harsher than I am about the mechanism section, on the grounds that a mechanism established by exclusion in *n* = 1 animal, with the direct ablation reported as having failed, is a hypothesis rather than a result. My own view is that the authors should get ahead of all three by name in the Discussion rather than leaving them to be discovered.
