# Review: "What the correlations between neurons say about each neuron" (Draft v4)

## 1. Summary

The paper asks whether the pairwise correlation matrix of a sampled neural population, with the stimulus discarded and no neuron labelled at test time, determines each neuron's preferred orientation and receptive-field position. A transformer reads the standardised correlation matrix of a sampled population, one row per neuron plus three row statistics, and emits a per-neuron orientation vector or an (x, y) position. On MICrONS (one mouse, 13 scans, 12,894 co-registered neurons) it reaches 25.4° mean angular error in vivo and 20.2° on the digital twin, against 45° for an uninformed decoder and 41.3° for the best constant guess, with R² = 0.23 and 0.48 for receptive fields; the readout is explicitly cardinal — 87%/79% correct cardinal axis against a 56% majority, 12°/19° within 15° of a cardinal axis, near chance at the obliques. The mechanism section shows that the class-averaged correlation matrix is nearly circulant, which fixes the orientation classes only up to D₈, and that a residual anisotropy (29% of class-Gram variance in vivo, 19% on the twin) supplies the absolute frame; the residual survives class balancing, disjoint stimulus halves and neuron-half resampling, is now described as one-peaked at 90° with same-class excesses given, and a model population carrying only a local random field over preferred orientation reproduces the cortical residual's shape (r = 0.39 to 0.48 with in-vivo V1) and fixes the frame in 15 of 15 runs across three residual sizes. Across 33 Allen mice, with relations from drifting gratings only, orientation transfers to held-out animals (≈36.9°) when populations mix animals and is 1.9° to 2.9° below chance — 0.8° to 1.8° below the best constant — when they do not; the paper attributes that to training diversity and supports it with a matched single-scan MICrONS run. Since v3 the substantive changes are: the dominant-orientation coincidence is restricted to V1, the residual's shape is measured and reported as one-peaked, the roadmap sentence and the limits paragraph now concede that the circulant/residual division of labour is inferred rather than measured, the synthetic anchor is replaced by a local field matched to cortex on residual shape and variance fraction, the complementary Gram-row-without-statistics ablation is run, the Allen margins are stated against both nulls with the best-constant line drawn in Figs. 2, 3 and 5, the v3 number errors are largely fixed, and two appendices, two figures and a section are cut, taking the paper from 21 to 19 pages.

## 2. Three strongest aspects

1. **The mechanism section now says exactly what it has and what it does not.** The roadmap sentence is fixed ("Four observations narrow down what fixes the frame. A fifth points to a specific anisotropy, which a model population then tests"), and the limits paragraph carries the concession in plain words: "No experiment removes the residual from the decoder's input, so the assignment of the ordering to the circulant part and the frame to the residual is inferred from the symmetry argument and the model, not measured." Together with the retained paragraph on the ablation that leaked labels and the retained failed cross-area rows, this is a mechanism section that a reader can audit. Three drafts ago the same section asserted a decomposition it had not tested.

2. **The residual's shape is now measured, and the model is matched to the shape rather than to a scalar.** v3 described the residual as "a cardinal anisotropy, stronger relations among neurons near the cardinal axes", which cannot break 0°/90°. v4 reports "Its form is one-peaked, not bi-cardinal" with the same-class excesses (+0.036 at 90° and −0.011 at 0° in vivo), replaces the Fourier-field model with a local field whose residual "has the one-peaked form of the cortical one and correlates 0.39 to 0.48 with the in-vivo V1 residual", and reports the model's residual variance fraction against cortex (0.05, 0.15, 0.23 against 0.16 twin and 0.30 in vivo). The earlier model is kept in one honest sentence, including the fact that killed it: "its residual correlates negatively with the cortical one ($-0.38$), so the local field is the model reported". Reporting the discarded model and the reason is the right behaviour and is rarer than it should be.

3. **The complementary ablation was run and the figures now carry the paper's own null.** "with the Gram row alone and no statistics it is $20.3^\circ$ on the twin and $26.5^\circ$ in vivo, raw and aligned, so the relations carry the frame and the scalars are dispensable" closes a question open since v2 in one training run, and it closes it in the direction that costs the authors something (26.5° against the standard 25.4° in vivo). The best-constant line is now drawn in Figs. 2, 3 and 5 — I checked the rendered PDFs — so Fig. 5's single-animal bars visibly sit just under the dotted 43.9° line instead of well under a 45° one. Methods' claim "Both lines are drawn in the figures" is true for the first time.

## 3. Major concerns

**3.1. The per-area coincidence, the paper's second headline claim, is now supported by one area and denied for two others by a bare assertion whose numbers have been deleted — and the denial reverses what the previous draft reported.**

The abstract: "in V1 the neurons preferring the dominant orientation co-fluctuate most strongly, and a model population with only that property, at the strength measured in cortex, recovers the frame". Section 3.2 gives V1 in full — "In V1, where 28\% of neurons prefer $90^\circ$, it peaks at $90^\circ$ on both substrates (factor 1.28 on the twin, 0.071 against 0.055; 1.77 in vivo, 0.095 against 0.054)" — which is what my v3 report asked for and is a real improvement.

The next sentence is the problem: "In RL and AL (1,125 and 294 neurons, label modes $0^\circ$) the peak class does not coincide with the label mode on either substrate, so the coincidence is established for V1 only." No peak class, no factor, no substrate breakdown. The reader is asked to accept a negative result about two of the four areas on the author's word, in a section whose entire argument is that a specific anisotropy is the anchor.

Two things make that unsatisfying rather than merely terse. First, v3 reported the opposite for AL on one substrate: the factor peaks "at $0^\circ$ in AL on the twin (1.49) and at $135^\circ$ in AL in vivo (1.69)", and AL's label mode is 0° (v2: "32\% of AL neurons prefer $0^\circ$"; v4: "label modes $0^\circ$"). On the twin, therefore, AL's peak class *was* its label mode. Either the analysis changed, or the new sentence is wrong. The paper does not say which, and the numbers that would settle it are the ones v4 removed. Second, the repository's own summary, updated in the same commit series, still reads "The dominant orientation of each area is also its most strongly co-correlated class (same-class mean correlation 0.071 vs 0.047–0.063 at $90^\circ$ in twin V1, 0.095 vs 0.046–0.082 in vivo, **0.204 at $0^\circ$ in AL**)". A referee without repository access would simply be unable to check; one with access finds the paper and the record in direct contradiction.

The deleted AL numbers also hid the more interesting problem, which v4 has not addressed: AL's anisotropy peaked 135° apart on the two substrates. The twin is a model fitted to the same recordings, and the mechanism's cleanest quantities (residual 0.16, factor 1.28, the 20.2° decoder) all come from it. If the anchor's *direction* is substrate-dependent in one area, that is evidence the anisotropy is partly a property of the fit, and it belongs in the limits paragraph next to "The twin substrate is a model fitted to the same recordings".

Finally, the anisotropy of the population the decoder actually reads is still never reported. Training and test populations mix all four areas, so the decoder sees a volume-wide residual; the paper reports the volume-wide residual's *size* (29%/19%) and V1's *direction*, but never the volume-wide peak class. With three of four areas now stated to peak somewhere other than their label mode, and V1–AL residual correlation at 0.31, the reader is left unable to tell whether the decoder reads one anchor or an average of four inconsistent ones — while Appendix B reports that "The per-area errors are within $3^\circ$ of each other", which on the face of it says all four areas are read in the same frame.

What would resolve it: a four-row table (V1, RL, LM, AL) giving, per substrate, the number of labelled neurons, the label mode and its fraction, the peak class of the anisotropy factor and the factor, plus one row for the whole volume. That is one table from quantities already computed, it makes the V1-only claim checkable, and it lets the reader see for themselves whether the anchor is area-specific. Add one sentence saying whether the AL twin/in-vivo disagreement of v3 survives the current analysis, and if it does, one sentence in the limits.

**3.2. The mechanism is named "co-fluctuation" and cited to the noise-correlation literature, but everything the paper measures is a signal correlation over trial-averaged responses — and on the twin there is no trial-to-trial variability at all.**

Methods is explicit about what the Gram is: "Each neuron's response vector is centred and normalised; the relation between two neurons is the cosine of their vectors, a signal correlation", where the in-vivo vector is "a trial-averaged in-vivo response to the oracle natural-movie clips (120 bins)" and the twin vector is a deterministic model output ("The twin substrate is cleaner, since the twin removes trial noise").

The mechanism is then named the other way round. Abstract: "in V1 the neurons preferring the dominant orientation **co-fluctuate** most strongly". Section 3.2: "the **co-fluctuation** of like-tuned neurons follows from their preferential connectivity \citep{ko2011} and from **the stimulus dependence of noise correlations** \citep{kohn2005}". Appendix B: "The anisotropy the cortical data show ... is modelled as **shared variability** among like-tuned neurons: **node noise** on 36 orientations, smoothed over the circle ...".

Trial-to-trial co-fluctuation is averaged out of the in-vivo Gram by construction and is absent from the twin by construction, yet the twin carries the anisotropy (factor 1.28, residual 0.16 of class-Gram variance) and gives the decoder its best cardinal accuracy (87%). So whatever the anisotropy is, it cannot be a noise correlation. What the paper has measured is that neurons preferring 90° have more similar *stimulus-driven* response profiles to one another than neurons preferring other orientations do — an anisotropy of tuning similarity, not of shared variability. The model is consistent with this (its field enters the response vector the Gram is computed from, so it acts as a shared stimulus-locked component), but its vocabulary — "node noise", "shared variability" — describes a different thing, and \citet{kohn2005} is cited in support of a reading the paper's own twin result rules out.

This is not a repair of the result; it is a repair of what the result is said to be, and it is in the abstract, in Section 3.2, in the Discussion ("the neurons preferring the over-represented orientation co-fluctuate most strongly") and in the appendix. As written, a reader will take the paper to have found an anisotropy in noise correlations, which would be a different and more surprising claim than the one the data support, and the citation to the noise-correlation literature will reinforce that reading.

What would resolve it: say what is measured — "neurons preferring 90° have more similar responses to one another than neurons of other orientations do", an anisotropy of signal correlations — in the abstract, Section 3.2 and the Discussion; in the model paragraph, describe the field as a shared stimulus-driven component over preferred orientation rather than as node noise or shared variability; keep \citet{ko2011} as a hypothesis about how like-tuned neurons come to have similar responses, and either drop \citet{kohn2005} or state explicitly that noise correlations are averaged out of this Gram and that the citation is offered only as a related anisotropy in a different quantity. If the authors do believe trial-to-trial co-fluctuation contributes in vivo, the test is one number: the same anisotropy factor computed on single-trial residuals rather than trial-averaged responses.

## 4. Minor issues

1. **"Four controls" but seven.** Appendix B opens "Four controls on the MICrONS standard configuration"; it contains seven paragraphs (What the decoder reads, Reference decoders, Cross-stimulus test, Scan and area, Class balance and priors, the subtraction ablation, Synthetic populations).

2. **"on every split the mixed populations of the same regime are $5^\circ$ to $8^\circ$ better" is wrong at the lower end.** From Table 2 the paired differences are 42.5−35.3 = 7.2, 42.9−37.2 = 5.7, 42.1−36.1 = 6.0, 43.1−35.2 = 7.9, 43.1−37.0 = 6.1 and 42.7−38.4 = 4.3. The range is 4.3° to 7.9°. This sentence is the paired comparison that now carries the single-animal/mixed contrast, so its bounds should be exact.

3. **The paired comparison in minor 2 is not size-matched.** Single-animal populations are 128 neurons, mixed 256 (Table 2's caption now says so). The matched control exists — mixed populations of 128 score 36.0° — but it appears only inside the pool-size argument. Point at it where the 4–8° contrast is claimed.

4. **The abstract rounds the Allen margin outward at both ends.** "$1^\circ$ to $2^\circ$ below the best constant guess" for values of 0.8° to 1.8°; Section 3.3 gives them correctly. Fig. 5's caption, which v3 flagged for the same reason, is now exact.

5. **The abstract rounds 24.5° down to "$24^\circ$".** Table 1's balanced twin error is 24.5°; the abstract says "($24^\circ$ balanced)", rounding in the favourable direction. 25° or 24.5°.

6. **"not at all" contradicts "weak but consistent signal".** Discussion: "inside a single Allen animal not at all". Section 3.3 and the abstract: a weak signal 0.8° to 1.8° below the best constant. Pick one characterisation.

7. **The twin oblique error is still quoted two ways, and a third way for both substrates.** Section 3.2: "$37^\circ$ to $40^\circ$ at the obliques on the twin". Appendix B: "oblique ones to $37^\circ$ to $39^\circ$ on the twin". Section 3.1: "$36^\circ$ to $39^\circ$". Fig. 3's twin bars are 36.8° and 39.4°, so 37–39 is right for the twin. Third round on this one.

8. **Two different residual variance fractions circulate without labels.** Section 3.2: "that residual holds 29\% of the class-Gram variance in vivo and 19\% on the twin" (whole volume — Fig. 4 confirms circulant variance 0.71/0.81). Table 4's note and the Section 3.2 model sentence use "0.16 on the twin, 0.30 in vivo" (V1). Mark each as volume-wide or V1 wherever it appears; as it stands the model comparison looks like it contradicts the section's opening paragraph.

9. **"and the rest less" is not true on the twin, by the same parenthesis.** "neurons preferring orientations within about $45^\circ$ of $90^\circ$ correlate more with each other than the circulant baseline predicts and the rest less (same-class excess $+0.036$ at $90^\circ$ and $-0.011$ at $0^\circ$ in vivo, $+0.014$ and $+0.006$ on the twin)" — on the twin both cardinals show a positive excess, and the asymmetry (0.008) is a sixth of the in-vivo one (0.047) although the twin gives the *better* cardinal readout (87% against 79%). State the claim as the ordering (excess at 90° exceeds excess at 0° on both substrates) and note that the twin's asymmetry is the smaller one.

10. **The stated anisotropy factor does not reproduce from its stated operands.** "1.28 on the twin, 0.071 against 0.055" gives 1.29. Either give one more digit or round the ratio from the unrounded operands.

11. **"In V1, where 28\% of neurons prefer $90^\circ$" does not say in what bin.** Appendix B's volume-wide figures use ±11° bins ("25\% of $90^\circ$"). State the binning, since the 28% is the only number behind the word "dominant".

12. **The model now fixes the frame at every strength run, so "at the strength measured in cortex" no longer marks anything.** Table 4: 5/5, 5/5, 5/5 at residual fractions 0.05, 0.15 and 0.23, the lowest being under a third of the twin's 0.16. v3's dose-response (2/5, 4/5, 5/5, 5/5) had a knee; v4's has none, because no failing strength was run. One or two weaker settings would restore the calibration, or the text should say that the property is sufficient throughout the range tested and that cortex sits well above the threshold.

13. **The model is an order of magnitude more decodable than cortex at a smaller residual.** 2° to 4° raw error at residual 0.05–0.23 against the cortical decoder's 20.2° at 0.16. That gap is worth one sentence: the model demonstrates sufficiency of the mechanism, not a quantitative match to cortex.

Without the ceiling the reader cannot tell whether 0.4 is good.

## 6. Recommendation

**Minor revision.**

v4 resolved three of v3's four major concerns with measurement rather than rewording — the residual's shape, the model matched on that shape with its variance fraction reported, the complementary ablation, the best-constant lines, the honest limits sentence — and it discarded a working model when its residual turned out to be anticorrelated with cortex, which is the same behaviour that made v3 credible. What remains is one claim supported by an unsupported negative (the per-area coincidence, whose RL/AL numbers were deleted rather than reported, and which reverses v3's own AL value) and one systematic mislabelling of the measured quantity as co-fluctuation or noise correlation when it is a signal correlation on trial-averaged and, on the twin, noiseless responses; both are fixable with one table, a handful of sentences and a citation, and I would not need to see the paper a fifth time.

**What a hostile reviewer 2 would say.** Four points. (i) Every MICrONS claim comes from one animal whose training and test halves share recording sessions, and the multi-animal result exists only for a designed 40-condition grating set — a tuning-profile similarity matrix, which is closer to classical RSA than to the paper's "discard the stimulus" premise — and vanishes on natural movies, so the paper's headline generalisation and its headline effect never co-occur in the same dataset. (ii) The decoder is a cardinal-axis classifier with a graded distance readout; obliques are at chance on both datasets and 63 to 72% of predictions land on two of eight classes, so "determines each neuron's preferred orientation" oversells a 25° mean, and the four-class Allen bar sits at the majority line. (iii) The mechanism section is exclusion plus a synthetic sufficiency demonstration in n = 1 animal: the direct ablation leaked labels and was withdrawn, the cross-area rotation was a single-seed artefact and was withdrawn, the anisotropy-versus-frame link is now conceded to be inferred rather than measured, and the model that carries the sufficiency claim fixes the frame at every strength tested — including a third of cortex's — at ten times cortex's accuracy, which reviewer 2 will read as a model that proves a mechanism is possible rather than that this one is operating. (iv) The area story has gone from "each area" to "V1 only" between drafts with the supporting numbers removed in the same edit, and reviewer 2 will ask to see the table (my 3.1). My own view: (i) and (ii) are conceded in the limits and should also be named in the Discussion's opening rather than only its closing paragraph; (iii) is answered by the reframing the paper has already largely done, plus minor 12; (iv) is answered by one table of quantities already computed.
